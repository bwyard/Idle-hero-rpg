## The Four Types

| Type | Focus |
|---|---|
| Combat | Strength, battle, adventurer combat performance |
| Merchant | Resources, logistics, trade, economic power |
| Knowledge | Ancient lore, research, magic, understanding |
| Hospitality | Relationships, alliances, reputation, coalition |

---

## Choosing and Changing

Guild type is chosen at founding. It can be changed at every prestige — it is one of
the decisions the player makes when passing the torch.

**Keeping the same type:** accumulated type bonuses carry forward and compound. The
longer a dynasty specializes in one type the stronger its type advantage becomes.

**Switching type:** accumulated bonuses from the previous type do not carry forward
to the new type. You start the new type at baseline. Switching has a real strategic
cost — a season of weakened Guild Rankings performance, lost type momentum — but
opens access to different strengths for the next generation.

Any guild master class can run any guild type. Natural synergies exist (a Warblade
running a Combat guild, a Diplomat running Hospitality) but nothing is locked. A
Diplomat running a Combat guild is a valid and interesting dynasty choice.

## How Guild Type Shapes the Game

### Guild Rankings
The four seasonal trial types (A/B/C/D — content TBD) map to guild type strengths.
Your type determines which trials you dominate and which you compensate for.
Switching types mid-dynasty shifts your trial performance accordingly.

### Survivor's Mark
Your guild type creates affinities toward certain trait templates. A Combat guild
accumulates different mark traits than a Hospitality guild even when making similar
choices. A dynasty that switches types carries a blended mark reflecting its full history.

### Pre-Demon-King Missions
The demon king quest chain includes preparation missions where different guild types
excel at different phases:

| Phase | Strong type | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Clearing the demonic vanguard | Combat | Direct military engagement |
| Recovering ancient lore | Knowledge | Research and investigation |
| Supplying allied guilds | Merchant | Logistics and resources |
| Building the coalition | Hospitality | Relationships and diplomacy |

No type is excluded from any phase. Weaker types can complete missions at higher
cost or slower pace. Players who specialize hard in one type will want to compensate
elsewhere before triggering the fight.

### Demon King Fight — Front Advantages
The three-front campaign maps to guild type strengths:

| Front | Strong type | Advantage |
|---|---|---|
| The Army | Combat | Combat depth and adventurer power |
| The Source | Knowledge | Ancient knowledge, demon king weakness intel |
| Allied guilds | Hospitality | More guilds respond, stronger allied contributions |
| Merchant | All fronts | Logistics layer — resource buffs across whichever front needs them |

A dynasty that deliberately builds type diversity across its history (e.g. Combat for
most runs, then Knowledge for a few before the fight) can approach all three fronts
from a position of strength. A single-type specialist will dominate one front and need
to compensate on the others.

---

## Strategic Implications

The most interesting long-term decision guild type creates: **when to specialize vs
when to diversify before the demon king fight.**

A player who stays Combat the whole dynasty will have the strongest Army front but
may struggle at the Source. Switching to Knowledge for 2-3 prestiges before triggering
the fight is a real strategic play — at the cost of dropping Guild Rankings performance
during that window.

This creates natural player archetypes:
- **Specialists** — pick a type and compound it, accept the fight will be uneven
- **Generalists** — rotate types deliberately to build balanced dynasty bonuses
- **Tacticians** — specialize early, pivot strategically before the fight
